# RFC 0001: TubmanTrain discovery charter

- Status: Draft
- Created: 2026-08-15
- Authors: iAUMO contributors
- Discussion: GitHub issue linked from this RFC after publication
- Decision owner: Project maintainer, with affected-community and domain-expert review required

## Summary

TubmanTrain is a working name for a community-governed initiative exploring how trusted networks might help people move knowledge, resources, and mutual support toward safety and opportunity.

This RFC starts a discovery process. It does not authorize a product, data collection, real-world operations, or use of Harriet Tubman's name as a brand. The project must first determine whether the concept is useful, safe, historically responsible, and wanted by the communities it might affect.

## Why an RFC comes first

The working name invokes Harriet Tubman and the Underground Railroad: a history of enslavement, resistance, courage, secrecy, collective action, and continuing public memory. A project using that reference can easily trivialize history, imply endorsement, or turn safety into a metaphor. The first responsibility is therefore consultation and definition—not software.

## Working hypothesis

A carefully governed, privacy-preserving coordination framework may help local groups share vetted knowledge and resources without centralizing sensitive information or exposing vulnerable people.

This hypothesis must be tested. "Framework" could ultimately mean a community practice, curriculum, directory, protocol, software tool, or a decision not to proceed.

## Principles

1. **Community authority.** People affected by the work participate in defining its goals, boundaries, and measures of success.
2. **Historical responsibility.** Historians and cultural stewards review the name, narrative, and educational claims before public promotion.
3. **Safety before reach.** Do not collect, publish, or infer sensitive personal or location data during discovery.
4. **Minimum necessary technology.** Prefer the least complex intervention that serves a validated need.
5. **No savior framing.** Design for reciprocal, accountable collaboration rather than rescue narratives.
6. **Transparent governance.** Record decisions, conflicts of interest, funding, and meaningful dissent.
7. **Accessible participation.** Provide plain-language materials and multiple ways to contribute.

## Non-goals for discovery

- Building or deploying a tracking, routing, identity, eligibility, or case-management system.
- Coordinating urgent assistance through GitHub.
- Collecting names, precise locations, immigration status, health information, legal matters, or other sensitive records.
- Claiming affiliation with the Tubman family, museums, parks, scholars, or community organizations.
- Treating historical language as a marketing device.

## Questions requiring expert and community review

### Purpose and beneficiaries

- What specific need is being addressed, for whom, and who identified it?
- What existing organizations already meet that need?
- Would new infrastructure help, duplicate, or disrupt existing work?

### Name and historical stewardship

- Is **TubmanTrain** respectful and accurate enough to continue as a working name?
- What permissions, consultation, attribution, and historical context are appropriate?
- What harms could the metaphor cause, and what alternative names should be considered?

### Safety and rights

- What threat models apply to participants, organizers, and communities?
- Can the need be met without collecting personal data?
- What accessibility, civil-rights, legal, and safeguarding review is required?

### Governance and sustainability

- Who may make which decisions, and how can affected people stop or change the work?
- How should contributions, funding, conflicts, and accountability be documented?
- What conditions should trigger a pause or project closure?

## Proposed discovery process

### Phase 0 — Stewardship review

- Invite historians, Tubman and Underground Railroad cultural institutions, and community stewards to review the concept and working name.
- Publish feedback with permission; record dissent and unresolved concerns.
- Define a clear proceed, rename, or stop decision.

Exit criterion: at least two independent cultural or historical reviewers and two affected-community representatives have been offered meaningful review, with no unresolved critical objection hidden from the public record.

### Phase 1 — Needs discovery

- Conduct compensated, consent-based listening sessions led or co-led by community partners.
- Map existing services before proposing a new intervention.
- Publish only aggregated, non-identifying findings.

Exit criterion: a validated problem statement, a harm analysis, and evidence that a new intervention would add value.

### Phase 2 — Options and threat model

- Compare non-technical and technical approaches.
- Complete privacy, security, accessibility, abuse, and operational risk reviews.
- Define governance, data boundaries, and a shutdown plan.

Exit criterion: a second RFC proposing a bounded pilot—or documenting a decision not to build.

## Decision policy

This RFC can be accepted only as a discovery charter. It cannot approve implementation. A later RFC must describe any pilot, its accountable operator, data practices, threat model, accessibility plan, evaluation, and exit conditions.

Strong objections about safety, historical stewardship, or affected-community consent are blocking until addressed in writing. Participation in a review does not imply endorsement.

## How to comment

Use the RFC review issue for public, non-sensitive feedback. Do not post personal cases or confidential information. Organizations or experts who need a private channel should wait until the maintainer publishes a valid, monitored contact method.
